## Bomb touch

Recounted on the day chain of 2026-11-19 (piece 8; measured only, nothing changed), replacing the
night's numbers. **The night's two numbers counted different units:** its table counted per
*carry* (one carry of a bomb, from getting it to losing it: 87 on the LAN, 32 of them with a
contact on the picture and no pass), its text per *seen contact* (each time a target was newly
drawn within the reach: 201, 182 of them without a pass within 500 ms). Now one unit everywhere:

An **attempt** is the scripted carrier's approach to one target, from the first frame its
picture shows the target within the pass reach (drawn distance ≤ 34 field units, game-core
`modes/bomb.ts`: 2 body radii + 2) to either a pass or 1 s without drawn contact (or the bombs
going off at a halving). The scripted player is the night's: a WebSocket client on an in-process
server that draws as the browser client does (the others interpolated 50 ms behind the newest
state, its own character at the newest state), reacts 250 ms late to its picture, chases the
nearest takeable opponent on it (other team, no bomb, not in the air) and dashes within 4 body
radii. Halving Bomb with the settings in force after piece 3 (the walls close in), seeds 1–10,
N = 10 matches per latency; latency one way each direction: LAN as is and +80 ms.

| Latency | Matches | Attempts | Passed (to that target) | Not passed | of those: 1 s without contact | bomb went to another | bombs went off |
| ------- | ------- | -------- | ----------------------- | ---------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------- | -------------- |
| LAN     | 10      | 317      | 92                      | 225        | 97                            | 119                  | 9              |
| +80 ms  | 10      | 296      | 76                      | 220        | 92                            | 118                  | 10             |

For every attempt not passed, the server's distance at the closest tick of the attempt (the
newest state the client had), whether the target was moving away then, and — where the server
had it within reach — why it did not pass:

| Latency | Not passed N | Closest distance median (min–max) | Within reach at the closest tick | Target fleeing | Return lock | Target in the air | Target carrying | Other |
| ------- | ------------ | --------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | -------------- | ----------- | ----------------- | --------------- | ----- |
| LAN     | 225          | 32.0 (1–55)                       | 153                              | 134            | 111         | 14                | 4               | 24    |
| +80 ms  | 220          | 32.2 (6–51.2)                     | 156                              | 142            | 117         | 7                 | 3               | 29    |

(Out of reach at the closest tick: 72 on the LAN, 64 at +80 ms.)

Reading: half of the attempts that did not pass were at the one who had just given the bomb —
the return lock (`passBack`, 1 s) keeps it from going back, and the client's picture does not
show that the giver may not take it, so a carrier (the script, and likely a player) chases it.
Of the rest, about a third were out of reach on the server at their closest (the target drawn in
the past, fleeing); a few targets were in the air (a jump) or carried a bomb by then. "Other"
(24 and 29) were within reach with none of these reasons at the moment measured — most likely the
bomb went to a nearer opponent in the same tick (the server passes to the nearest), which the
end column counts as "bomb went to another". Latency changes little: the passes fall from 92 to
76 of about 300 attempts.
